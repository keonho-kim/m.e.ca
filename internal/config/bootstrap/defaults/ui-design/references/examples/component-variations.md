# Derive a composition from context

Use these contrasting situations to decide what changes when use changes. They are not mappings from a domain to a mandatory component or copied asset. Infer the user's real task, state assumptions and adapt reliable primitives. The catalog does not need to contain the resulting arrangement or give it a name.

## The same prices, different decisions

**Occasional inspection:** a person checks a total and sometimes consults its explanation. A stable total with optional line-item disclosure may fit; Price Breakdown is one possible expression.

**Comparison:** a person needs several costs and conditions together before choosing. Keep relevant amounts visible in aligned rows, open groups or a side-by-side comparison. Do not require repeated opening and remembering merely to reproduce the accordion recipe. Preserve real units, scope and quote state; invented fees, discounts and decorative count-up cannot improve the decision.

Check whether the needed facts can be compared without losing context. The preferable arrangement can change with content volume, width and how often the comparison repeats.

## The same queue, different work patterns

**Repeated processing:** a stable table/list with nearby actions, useful density, retained query and keyboard operation may reduce repeated travel. Avoid replayed entrances and obligatory confirmations for ordinary reversible work.

**Unfamiliar or consequential input:** clearer grouping, relevant examples or a review may help. A stepper earns its place through actual dependencies, not unfamiliarity alone. Both surfaces can share type, material and control semantics without identical density or layout.

Do not invent a KPI opening, activity feed or promotional hero to fill either screen. Verify the actual task with representative records; inferred familiarity is an assumption unless supported.

## Peer views versus simultaneous evidence

Tabs can work when someone visits independent views in one context. If the decision needs evidence from multiple views simultaneously, consider a split region, pinned summary or combined comparison instead. A narrower desktop may need a deliberate alternate arrangement with retained selection, rather than unreadably squeezed columns.

The design can combine a stable summary with local filters and optional detail, even if no named recipe describes that composition. Check reading order, memory burden, keyboard focus, stale data and the true applied scope before refining an underline or panel transition.

## The same action label, different consequences

A reversible preference save can use local pending/confirmed feedback; a recurring acknowledgement may not need a toast. A booking or other consequential commitment with an uncertain response may need persistent status, actual identity and supported reconciliation. A completed-looking step or success animation cannot decide its outcome.

Choose review, interruption and recovery by actual risk and reversibility. Do not apply a confirmation dialog to every Save, or make every failure disappear after a display timer. Check that valid input survives and that recovery cannot blindly duplicate the operation.

## Sequence, independence and background work

Source selection, dependent mapping and final commitment can form genuine stages. Independent configuration may fit grouped sections with a persistent summary and commit region instead. Background processing needs actual status, cancellation semantics and results; a wizard's stage count is not job progress.

Combining a small stage navigator with a real processing/result region is valid when each has its own purpose. Do not add optional stages or fake telemetry to make the flow seem sophisticated. Verify back, changed dependencies, partial failure and return to ongoing work.

## Expression follows the same reasoning

A public product story with genuine imagery may benefit from a composed reveal or manual gallery. Frequently repeated operational navigation may need immediate replacement. Both can retain the same glass, minimal, soft or futuristic identity. Motion should clarify something specific, preserve operation and have an equivalent reduced-motion path.

An explicit expressive brief deserves a fitting interpretation; anti-slop is not a reason to erase it. Conversely, Blinds, Depth Stack, an unfamiliar shape or fewer detector warnings do not by themselves improve comprehension. Check actual content, hierarchy and the task before polishing the effect.

## The same neutral base, different emphasis

An operational view can distribute strong accents across action, selected context and actual status. A content library may additionally use stable resource-category color while imagery carries most variation. Editorial detail can make title/media/body the main hierarchy and keep tools quieter. None requires a different brand on every route.

Choose meanings and paired contrast before swatches. Minimalism is compatible with visible accents; monochrome treatment can also obscure differences. Reuse the target system's roles rather than importing the study's exact hues or assigning arbitrary colors to every item.

## The same side pane, different scope

A global application rail, project mode list, schema tree, document outline and filmstrip can look similar but answer different questions. A shallow global set may move above the work while the content tree stays beside it. Avoid both repeating sidebars everywhere and removing a useful object pane just to appear different.

## Revise when the assumption changes

For a material choice, explain context → difficulty → arrangement → expected benefit/cost → check. A short agent walkthrough can expose hidden comparison or lost input, but it is not proof of user preference. Use actual feedback and observed behavior to revise the composition, including abandoning a catalog example. Do not demand a user-supplied design vocabulary or create a new mandatory command.
