# Use glass to explain a real layer

Choose transparency when retained background context helps orientation or content inspection. It is a material decision, not a complete app layout, a corporate preset or a requirement for a creative/technical category.

## Assign a useful role

A navigation/tool layer can retain context while scrolling; a contextual inspector can sit over relevant work; a public/media composition can use one deliberate overlay. Dense forms, code, tables and sustained reading usually need calm high-opacity work surfaces. Keep their type, boundaries and actions clear.

Do not add a decorative gradient simply to have something to blur. A background should contribute actual content, orientation or a coherent environment. Avoid repeated glass cards nested inside glass panels, unrelated light blobs and translucent functional text.

## Build hierarchy before blur

Define backdrop, surface/tint, opaque fallback, primary/supporting ink, boundary, elevation and focus roles in the target system. Use proportion, typography and grouping to establish the task before tuning opacity. A lit edge or elevation can clarify the layer without giving every row both a wide shadow and border.

Neutral material can support several role-based accents. Keep action, selection, category and real status meanings stable rather than changing chrome to match every photograph. Light and dark glass are separate relationships chosen for the actual scene, not inverted versions of one palette.

## Verify on the actual background

Check foreground/controls over the brightest, darkest and most detailed relevant positions, including selected, focused and error states. Token contrast against a tint alone cannot establish the composite. Increase opacity or use an opaque area when context makes reading harder.

Provide a readable feature fallback, and inspect reduced-transparency, increased-contrast and forced-color behavior where supported. The hierarchy and control state should remain understandable without blur. Menus need usable placement and focus rather than a clipped effect.

## Keep motion and cost bounded

Glass does not require animated backdrop blur, pointer-following light or a full-window moving field. Use state-driven local motion where it clarifies the operation. Measure paint/compositing and interaction before multiplying filtered layers or adding a new dependency.

Inspect real content at intended widths/heights, zoom, overlays and available reduced-effects conditions. A frosted screenshot alone is not evidence that controls, loading or fallback work.
