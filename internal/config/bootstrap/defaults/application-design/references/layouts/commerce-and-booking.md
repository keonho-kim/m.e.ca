# Lead with the facts that establish a choice

A catalog can support discovery without checkout. An order manager may begin with a table. Booking may start from atmosphere, a resource or time. Select the path from the person's decision rather than a commerce template.

## Discovery and detail

Use meaningful scope/categories, useful filters, real item summaries and clear detail destinations. Visual collections suit appearance-led exploration; aligned rows suit repeated-field comparison. Make condition, capacity, price/unit and availability visible where they affect the choice.

Detail can be editorial when imagery and narrative deserve sustained attention. Keep decisive facts and a supported action near the relevant content; a large photo can improve inspection while pushing metadata below the opening. Explain that tradeoff when comparison is frequent.

A gallery, reviews, stock, delivery and related items are conditional. Do not invent scarcity, discounts, popularity or testimonials to fill the composition.

## Commitment and result

Retain the selected object/service and actual scope, the information needed to decide, supported input and a clear commitment. Present real conditions and a useful summary before a consequential action. Cart, payment, consent and personal-data fields follow the actual product, not the example's page count.

Pending, confirmed, failed, partial and uncertain outcomes need different feedback. Preserve valid input and a route to the result or recovery. A timeout can follow a completed operation; a polished confirmation does not authorize blind replay.

## Time/resource work

Show date/period, resource, timezone and actual units. Choose a calendar, agenda, comparable resource/time rows or a small slot set according to volume and the comparison needed. A decorative calendar with invented events is not an availability feature.

Keep current selection unambiguous. A desktop can show schedule and summary together; small windows need a deliberate alternative without losing the chosen resource/time. Make unavailable choices explainable and provide non-gesture/keyboard access.

Recurrence, capacity, waitlists and reminders belong only when supported. Visible availability is not a reservation; a real service confirms the commitment and reconciles conflicts while preserving valid choices.

## Visual emphasis and feedback

Use content scale, aligned values, clear selected state and nearby actions. Distribute accent where category, selection or actual outcome needs recognition; do not color every cell or treat a selected time as a completed booking. Neutral, warm, dark or tactile material remains subordinate to the decision.

Use known geometry for fetched catalog/availability regions. Retain correctly identified scope and input during refresh/commitment; do not relabel the previous resource's availability. Use [loading](../loading-and-progress.md), [forms](../components/forms.md) and genuine [stages](../components/steppers.md) when applicable.

Check no matches, long conditions, stale availability, unavailable values, conflict, lost confirmation and return. A frontend-only study demonstrates arrangement and local behavior, not commercial, legal, authorization or concurrency guarantees for a real service.
